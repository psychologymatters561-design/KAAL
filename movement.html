01 · Why this page exists

Every other part of a watch can be photographed

You can judge a case from a picture. You can judge a dial, a bezel, a bracelet, the way light moves across a finish. Hold the photograph up and the claim is either true or it is not.

The movement is the one component that does not work that way. It sits behind the dial, it never comes out, and no photograph of the outside of a watch tells you anything about it. It is also the only part that is doing any work — everything else is housing.

Which leaves a name as the only evidence available. So most brands at this price simply do not give one. The specification says "quartz movement" and stops, and the reader is invited not to notice that the sentence has no subject.

KAAL gives the name. Not as a feature, as the minimum.

02 · What is inside Series 01

A Seiko Epson calibre, from Japan

A Japanese Seiko Epson multi function quartz movement, in all twenty pieces, backed for twenty four months from dispatch.

The same movement in number 01 and in number 20. No tiering, no better version of this watch held back for a later series.

Seiko has been making clocks and watches in Japan since 1892, and is one of a very short list of companies anywhere that makes an entire watch — movement, case, dial, hands — under its own roof. Seiko Epson is the arm of that group which supplies movements to other watchmakers.

That is the whole reason to use one. A small independent brand has two honest options for the part it cannot manufacture: buy the best engine available at the price, or build a worse one and hope the dial distracts you. Buying from Seiko Epson is the first option, and naming it is the only way anybody outside the workshop can tell which option was taken.

03 · Reading the three hands

Three complications, and what each one is for

Multi function quartz means one movement driving more than the time. Series 01 uses three, and they are worth knowing because a watch you cannot read is a bracelet.

The day ring

The day of the week, shown on its own track rather than in a cut-out window. It advances once a day and needs nothing from you.

The twenty four hour counter

A small hand that makes one full turn a day instead of two. It exists so that ten past three in the morning and ten past three in the afternoon are not the same reading — which matters if you travel, if you keep unusual hours, or if you have ever set an alarm twelve hours wrong.

The pointer date

The date, indicated by a hand sweeping a printed scale rather than punched through the dial in a window. It is the harder way to show a date and the better looking one: nothing is cut out of the dial, so the face stays whole.

Set them once when the watch arrives and the movement carries all three from then on.

04 · Quartz, on purpose

Naming the movement, rather than naming a word

A fair question from anyone who knows watches: at this price, why not an automatic?

Because of what the answer would have to be. A named Japanese automatic movement costs a meaningful fraction of ₹5,999 on its own, before a case, a dial, a bracelet, hand finishing, inspection, a box and delivery. Any brand selling an automatic at this price is either losing money on every piece or fitting a movement it has decided not to name.

So the choice was never quartz against automatic. It was a movement we can name against a word we can print. "Automatic" on a dial is a word. "Seiko Epson" is a supplier with an address, a century of movements behind it, and a reputation it will not lend to something that does not work.

What quartz gives you
Accuracy measured in seconds a monthNo winding, no daily rate to chase, no service interval, and it keeps time whether you wear it every day or twice a month.
What it costs you
A battery every few yearsStated plainly because it is the honest trade. There is no rotor and no sweep of a mechanical seconds hand. If those are what you want from a watch, this is not the watch, and we would rather you knew that now than in seven days.

05 · The twenty four months

What is covered, in the same words for everybody

Twenty four months on the movement, from the date of dispatch. If the movement fails inside that window, KAAL repairs or replaces it.

It is twenty four months and not twelve because a movement that is going to fail fails early, and a brand that believes its own supplier can afford to say so. Two years is long enough that the promise costs something to make.

Not covered: cosmetic damage, water ingress from a case opened by anyone other than us, and normal wear to the bracelet and glass. Those are exclusions and they are listed here rather than buried, because a warranty whose limits you discover at the point of claim is not a warranty.

Nothing in it limits any right you have under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. The complete terms are on the legal page, and they are the same terms for all twenty buyers.

Yes. The movement is a Seiko Epson calibre, Japanese. Seiko Epson is the Seiko Group company that supplies watch movements to other watchmakers.

Is KAAL Series 01 quartz or automatic?

Quartz, and deliberately so. At ₹5,999 an automatic would mean an unnamed movement, because a named Japanese automatic costs more than the whole watch. KAAL would rather name the movement than use the word automatic.

How accurate is the movement?

A quartz movement of this class keeps time to within seconds a month. A mechanical movement at any price is doing well to stay inside ten seconds a day.
