01 · Why this page exists

Every other part of a watch can be photographed

You can judge a case from a picture. You can judge a dial, a bezel, a bracelet, the way light moves across a finish. Hold the photograph up and the claim is either true or it is not.

The movement is the one component that does not work that way. It sits behind the dial, it never comes out, and no photograph of the outside of a watch tells you anything about it. It is also the only part that is doing any work — everything else is housing.

Which leaves a name as the only evidence available. So most brands at this price simply do not give one. The specification says "quartz movement" and stops, and the reader is invited not to notice that the sentence has no subject.

KAAL gives the name. Not as a feature, as the minimum.

The three registers are not a styling decision. They are where the VX7N puts its hand posts, so the dial is drawn around the movement rather than the movement squeezed behind a dial — which is why the day, the twenty four hour hand and the date each get a full sub-register instead of a window punched through the face.

On the company, without the flourish

Seiko Epson is the Seiko Group company that manufactures movements, at Shiojiri in Nagano. At the far end of its range it is where Spring Drive and the Grand Seiko 9F quartz are built — calibres that cost more than this entire series.

The VX7N is not one of those, and this page is not going to imply that it is.

What it is: the volume calibre of a firm that cannot afford a reputation for movements that stop. That is the whole argument for buying a movement instead of building one. A small brand's two honest options are to fit the best engine it can buy at the price, or to fit a worse one and hope the dial distracts you — and naming the calibre is the only way anyone outside the workshop can tell which was done.

03 · Reading the three registers

Five hands, and what each one is for

Multi function means one movement driving more than the time. The VX7N drives five hands, and the three beyond the obvious are worth knowing, because a watch you cannot read is a bracelet.

Day, at twelve

The day of the week on its own track at the top of the dial, rather than in a cut-out window. It advances once a day and asks nothing of you.

Twenty four hour, at nine

A hand making one full turn a day instead of two. It exists so that ten past three in the morning and ten past three in the afternoon are not the same reading — which matters if you travel, keep unusual hours, or have ever set an alarm twelve hours wrong.

Pointer date, at six

The date indicated by a hand sweeping a printed scale rather than punched through the dial in a window. It is the harder way to show a date and the better looking one: nothing is cut out of the face, so the dial stays whole.

Set them once when the watch arrives and the movement carries all three from then on.

04 · How it keeps time

Thirty two thousand seven hundred and sixty eight

Inside the VX7N is a sliver of quartz, cut in the shape of a tuning fork, small enough to sit inside a metal can about the size of a grain of rice.

Put a voltage across quartz and it deforms. Bend quartz and it produces a voltage. That two way relationship is the piezoelectric effect, and it lets a circuit drive the fork and read its vibration at the same time. The fork has one frequency it wants to vibrate at, decided by how it was cut, and it holds that frequency stubbornly — far more stubbornly than a balance wheel and hairspring, which are a mass on a spring and are affected by gravity, by position, by wear and by how long since it was last serviced.

Why that exact number

The fork runs at 32,768 vibrations per second. It is not a round number by accident — it is 215, and that is the entire reason it was chosen.

A circuit cannot easily divide by 32,000. It can divide by two, trivially, using one flip-flop. So the movement chains fifteen of them: 32,768 halves to 16,384, halves to 8,192, and thirteen more halvings later the count arrives at exactly 1. One pulse per second, derived with no arithmetic, no rounding and no remainder — which is the part that matters, because a rounding error repeated 86,400 times a day is not an error, it is a broken watch.

That single pulse drives a stepper motor, the motor turns the gear train, and the gear train moves five hands. Everything downstream of the fork is ordinary. The precision was decided at the oscillator.

Seconds a dayTen to thirty seconds a day is ordinary, and a certified chronometer is permitted −4 to +6. The gap is roughly thirtyfold, and it is not a matter of price — it is the physics of a spring against the physics of a crystal.

One honest weakness, since the rest of this page is honest: quartz frequency moves slightly with temperature. The fork is cut so its flattest point sits near the temperature of a wrist, which is why a watch that is worn keeps better time than one left in a hot car. That is the trade, stated rather than omitted.

05 · Quartz, on purpose

Naming the movement, rather than naming a word

A fair question from anyone who knows watches: at this price, why not an automatic?

Because of what the answer would have to be. A named Japanese automatic movement costs a meaningful fraction of ₹5,999 on its own, before a case, a dial, a bracelet, hand finishing, inspection, a box and delivery. Any brand selling an automatic at this price is either losing money on every piece or fitting a movement it has decided not to name.

There is also the matter, set out above, that the quartz is simply the more accurate machine by a factor of about thirty. An automatic is chosen for what it is — a spring, a rotor, a craft object that runs on your own movement — and those are good reasons. Keeping better time is not among them.

So the choice was never quartz against automatic. It was a movement we can name against a word we can print. "Automatic" on a dial is a word. "Seiko Epson VX7N" is a part number: a supplier, an address in Nagano, a specification sheet, and a movement any watchmaker in the country can identify, service and price without asking us anything.

What quartz gives you
Accuracy measured in seconds a monthNo winding, no daily rate to chase, no service interval, and it keeps time whether you wear it every day or twice a month.
What it costs you
A cell about every three yearsStated plainly because it is the honest trade. There is no rotor, and no sweep of a mechanical seconds hand — the VX7N ticks once a second, visibly. If those are what you want from a watch, this is not the watch, and we would rather you knew now than in seven days.

06 · The twenty four months

What is covered, in the same words for everybody

Twenty four months on the VX7N, from the date of dispatch. If the movement fails inside that window, KAAL repairs or replaces it.

It is twenty four months and not twelve because a movement that is going to fail fails early, and a brand that believes its own supplier can afford to say so. Two years is long enough that the promise costs something to make.

Not covered: cosmetic damage, water ingress from a case opened by anyone other than us, and normal wear to the bracelet and glass. Those are exclusions and they are listed here rather than buried, because a warranty whose limits you discover at the point of claim is not a warranty.
